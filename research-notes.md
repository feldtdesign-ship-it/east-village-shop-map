## 2. Vacancy
- CD3 (LES+EV) storefront vacancy 15.4% Oct 2025, down 8.35% vs Oct 2023 (Comptroller/Storefront tracker)
- Citywide 11%, ~15,700 empty (Comptroller, Jun 2026); 80–90% of vacants empty 9+ months
- SBS 2019 inventory: 1,767 storefronts, 14.4% vacant; by corridor: St Marks 11%, 1st Ave 12%, 2nd Ave 15%, Ave C 16%, Ave D 17%, Ave A 18%, Ave B 25%
- 2019–21: 336 EV businesses closed
- 2026 chain arrivals: Sephora 7,800 SF at 3rd Ave & St Marks (opened May 2026), Barnes & Noble (2026), Metro Acres grocery (Mar 2026), ATANY made-to-order tees 316 Bowery → corridor moving upmarket at the west end

## 3. Business mix & numbers (SBS CDNA, data 2019–2020)
- Storefront mix: 295 full-service restaurants, 155 bars/liquor, 142 beauty/nail/barber, 101 limited-service, 94 coffee/cafes, 59 clothing/shoe/jewelry, 50 gifts/books, 35 galleries/theaters/art, 44 smoke/tattoo
- Residents spend $3.66B/yr; local businesses make $3.32B retail sales; $337M leaks out (grocery, gas, dept stores)
- Clothing stores SURPLUS $249.2M (EV clothing stores sell $249M more than residents spend → destination/visitor draw). Shoe stores surplus $92.6M. Restaurants surplus $502M. Book/music $57.8M surplus.
- EV total business sales $703.6M (2020, tax data), +49% 2013–20. Median sales per business $173,839 (2020).
- Merchants: 95% rent; median 9 yrs in business; median 2 FT employees; 33% improved / 21% decreased last year; top ask = marketing support (27%)
- Shoppers: 84% shop daily; 72% arrive on foot, 13% subway, 7% bike; 59% no set time, 26% weekdays
- Shoppers want: independent local stores, cafes, CLOTHING stores (#3 ask)
- TransAlt 2012: pedestrians spend $158/wk, cyclists $163, subway $111, car $143; 95% arrive without car; two-thirds of walkers visit 5+ times/wk
- Named-store estimates (Datanyze/Owler, low confidence): Trash & Vaudeville ~$1.4M rev, 9 staff; L Train Vintage ~$763k, 6 staff
- Benchmarks: specialty apparel ~$400/SF/yr sales; Brooklyn 500 SF boutique grossed $267k (~$535/SF)

## 4. Demographics
- EV neighborhood (SBS/ACS 2014–18): 62,179 pop; density 96k/sq mi; median age 34.4; 43% aged 25–44; 58% bachelor's+; MHI $57,104; 24% below poverty (public housing east of Ave C skews low); HH size 1.83; 16% car ownership
- Private estimates 2024–25: MHI $106k (Niche) to $124k (Point2) — reflects west/central EV renters, not Alphabet City
- Barbell: high-earning 25–44 renters west of Ave B + long-tenured lower-income east of Ave C
- Commuting: 27,580 live in EV & work elsewhere; 15,544 work in EV; 888 both
- Jobs in EV: 39% food/accommodation/arts, 21% education/health (NYU, Cooper Union, Mt Sinai Beth Israel area)
- CD3 PUMA 2024 MHI $59,411 (DataUSA); Furman LES/Chinatown 2024 MHI $53,970, poverty 27.1%

## 6. Visitors & seasonality
- NYC 2025: 65.0M visitors (52.4M domestic, 12.5M intl); $55.6B direct spend; 2026 forecast 66.3M; ~8M visitors Thanksgiving–New Year
- Holiday (Nov–Dec) ≈ 19% of annual US retail sales (NRF); apparel +6.1% YoY Dec 2025
- EV is a "lifestyle corridor" (Placer.ai typology): peaks spring/summer + December, not December-only like SoHo
- August weakest (pop-up data); Feb/Sep fashion weeks lift
- Events: Ukrainian Festival E 7th (May 15–17, 2026); Dance Parade → Tompkins (May 16); Loisaida Festival Ave C (May 24); Halloween Dog Parade Tompkins (Oct 19); NYU move-in late Aug, graduation May; World Cup Jun–Jul 2026 (+1.2M visitors)
- St Marks 2nd–3rd Ave block = most tourist-oriented; E 9th 1st–2nd = local/boutique destination
