Where an apparel & art shop can actually pencil in the East Village

One map of what is for rent, who is already selling clothes and art, who walks these blocks, and when. Built from live listings, the city's East Village needs assessment, MTA ridership, and NYC Tourism data. Pins are placed at block level from street addresses; confirm exact frontage on a walk.
1

E 9th St, 1st–2nd Ave

The clothing cluster: 17+ boutiques and vintage shops on one block, ask rents near $96–130/SF, one block from the 1st Ave L. Shoppers already come here to buy clothes.

2

St Marks Pl, 2nd Ave → Ave A

Highest walk-by volume: tourists, NYU, nightlife. Asks $160–225/SF. Sephora and Barnes & Noble arrived at the 3rd Ave end in 2026, so rents are heading up.

3

Avenue A, 5th–11th (Tompkins frontage)

Local disposable income and evening traffic, roughly $100–150/SF, and enough vacancy (18% pre-COVID) to negotiate. Weaker daytime tourist flow.

The rent math is the whole decision. Independent apparel stores sell about $400–535 per square foot a year. At a St Marks ask of $200/SF, rent alone is 40–50% of typical sales. Only the ~$100/SF band (E 9th, E 7th, upper Ave A, side streets) lets floor sales carry the lease at a normal 20–25% occupancy cost. Anything pricier needs online, wholesale or event revenue on top, or a smaller room (400–750 SF).

      Who shops here

      Riders

      Locals: two neighborhoods in one

      62,000 residents at 96,000 per square mile. West of Avenue B: renters aged 25–44 (43% of the population), 58% with a degree, private estimates put median household income at $106k–124k. East of Avenue C: long-tenured, lower-income households in public housing pull the official median down to $57k and poverty to 24%. Household size is 1.83; only 16% own a car.

      Daytime: workers and students

      15,500 people work in the East Village; 39% of those jobs are food, bars and arts, 21% education and health. Cooper Union sits at the west edge and NYU keeps adding dorms (it bought 35 Cooper Square in 2024). Students thin out mid-May to late August.

      Visitors

      NYC drew 65M visitors in 2025 (52.4M domestic, 12.5M international) and forecasts 66.3M for 2026. The East Village is not a top-ten tourist stop; St Marks between 3rd and 2nd is the one block that behaves like one. Visitors arrive from the tri-state area, Philadelphia, DC, LA and Boston.

      When they shop

      Service calendar 2026

      The East Village behaves like what Placer.ai calls a lifestyle corridor: traffic peaks in spring and early summer and again in December, rather than December only like SoHo. Nationally, November–December carry about 19% of annual retail sales. Pop-up operators here price August lowest and fashion weeks (February, September) and the holidays highest.

      How to read the risks

      Service changes

      Working for you

      • Clothing is a proven destination category here (+$249M surplus).
      • Vacancy is falling: 15.4% across LES/EV in Oct 2025, down from 2023. Landlords still deal.
      • Shoppers walk and come daily. A good window sells by itself.
      • Shoppers explicitly asked for more clothing stores and galleries.

      Working against you

      • Prime asks are 2–3× what floor sales support. Negotiate on the "price on request" spaces.
      • Chains are landing at the 3rd Ave end; expect rent pressure to move east along St Marks.
      • 336 East Village businesses closed 2019–21. Median tenure is 9 years; 95% of merchants rent.
      • Summer and January are soft; students leave; locals travel. Plan cash for a 20–30% dip.
